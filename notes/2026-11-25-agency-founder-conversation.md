# Conversation notes: agency founder on virality, storytelling and going all-in

> **Off the record.** The guest agreed to a recording for personal reference only, and asked to "wait and see" before anything is posted. Don't publish or quote them publicly without asking first.

## The big ideas

1. **Virality is a byproduct, not a goal.** It's "lightning in a bottle." You can catch it if you take enough swings, but if virality is your bar for success you will always be disappointed.
2. **Start from the business goal.** Every campaign needs an objective, and the objective decides the metrics. *Reach new audiences* (e.g. Gen Z parents who don't consider the brand yet) and *drive purchase* (get them into the store) are two different campaigns, measured two different ways.
3. **Always know who you're making it for.** If you're making art for yourself, post whatever you want. If you want commercial success or a business, ask what story will land with your typical viewer, and tell it that way.
4. **Creativity comes from practice.** There's no single right answer. Make many versions, see what works, and do it again.
5. **Take the leap and expect it to be messy.** You don't need to know everything first. Most people stay in a narrow lane because they won't take the risk.

## How an agency gets from a brief to an idea

| Step | What happens | Example (diaper brand reaching Gen Z parents) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Brief** | The client brings goals, key considerations, timeline and budget | "Reach Gen Z parents this holiday season" |
| **2. Strategy & consumer insight** | Find out what the audience is already saying and what's true for them | New parents are stressed and short on time |
| **3. Positioning** | Connect the product to that truth | The brand as a *stress reducer*: one less thing to worry about |
| **4. Creative idea** | Find a creative way to tell that story | A competition between parents where the one using the brand stays calm |
| **5. Options** | Pitch **3–5 different ideas** | Contest, award show, … |
| **6. Narrow & revise** | Pick one with the client and revise it a few times | "Not a contest. What about an award show?" |
| **7. Media plan** | Choose the platforms (a specialty of its own) | Gen Z → TikTok/YouTube; Boomers → Facebook/TV |

**Platforms:** some brands post the same asset everywhere. With more time and budget, make a version for each platform that follows that platform's norms.

**Measuring success:** it's a matter of degree. Benchmarks and measurement are a science of their own, and experts will still disagree. Five experts would give five different reasons a campaign worked, and you can't A/B test this kind of creative work. It's part art, part science.

## Advice for my situation

**On leaving the corporate job**
- Be practical about money: have **6–12 months of runway**, then go. That means 6–12 months, not "6–12 months plus two more years of saving."
- There's never a good time, so don't wait for a bigger cushion.
- The downside is small: if month 9 isn't working, go back to a bank job. "Who cares?"
- Side projects while you have income are fine, but at some point you have to make the leap.
- "In 3 years you'll be surprised where you end up, and it's probably not what you think." Start → learn → pivot → get traction.
- The guest had never worked at an agency before starting one, and learned mostly on the job. They look back at early work and think "wow, I was so ignorant." That's what growing looks like.

**On fear of posting and being judged**
- Some friends and colleagues *will* take shots at you ("she's egotistical"). That says more about them than about you. People who feel stuck often get grumpy at people who take risks.
- Mean comments from strangers are the same: it's their mood, not your problem.
- Thick skin comes from repetition. It keeps happening and you find out you're okay.
- Reframe: your favorite artist wakes up to a million haters every day. Being visible always means haters. You can stay small because of them, or go after the dream and be proud of it.
- **Don't read the comments.** (The guest has never read the negative reviews of their own company.)

**On editing and "what story do I tell?"** (e.g. the concert footage)
- I was looking for *the right answer*. There isn't one, so **just pick one.**
- Choose based on **the audience**: picture one typical viewer (e.g. a woman my age in Manhattan questioning her career) and ask what story would make *her* watch and share it.
- That choice is what tells you which clips to keep, and what to shoot next time.

## Practice plan (straight from the advice)

> "Shoot the concert, then tell 5 different versions of it."

- [ ] Take one shoot (e.g. the concert) and cut **5 versions**, one a day, Monday to Friday:
  - [ ] Milestone angle (e.g. the artist's first time at a big venue)
  - [ ] Crowd / fan-experience angle
  - [ ] Personal angle (why I was there; career-questioning me)
  - [ ] Behind-the-lens / how I shot it
  - [ ] Short-form hook (≤15s) vs. a long-form cut (~5 min)
- [ ] Note which ones *I* liked and which ones *others* liked.
- [ ] Repeat the next week with new footage. Think "100 videos in 100 days."
- [ ] Before each edit, write one line: **"This is for ___, and the story is ___."**
- [ ] Send the best cut to the artist's team with a pitch to shoot a future show.
- [ ] Post something even though it's scary.
